# Week 5 Recap — 2026-11-20 (Days 20–24)

## This week in one paragraph
This was the Black–Scholes week. Monday finished the derivation: the Δ-hedged portfolio is riskless, so it must earn r, which gives **∂f/∂t + rS f_S + ½σ²S² f_SS = rf**, an equation with no μ, satisfied by every derivative (checked on a forward). Tuesday used the missing μ to price in a risk-neutral world and got **c = S₀N(d₁) − Ke^{−rT}N(d₂)**, confirmed by Monte Carlo and by plugging the formula back into the PDE. Wednesday read the formula: **N(d₂)** is the risk-neutral exercise probability, and **N(d₁)** is delta. Thursday turned the formula around to get **implied volatility**, and handled **dividends** (subtract their PV, or use S₀e^{−qT}). Friday reviewed Module C by pricing one call three ways (tree, PDE, risk-neutral expectation) and getting **7.787** each time.

## Day by day
**Day 20 — The BSM differential equation (part 2)** (Mon)
- ΔΠ = rΠΔt ⇒ ∂f/∂t + rS ∂f/∂S + ½σ²S² ∂²f/∂S² = rf.
- No μ. The derivative is fixed by its boundary condition (payoff at T).
- Forward f = S − Ke^{−r(T−t)} satisfies it; S² doesn't (residual (r + σ²)S²); e^{(r+σ²)(T−t)}S² does.

**Day 21 — Risk-neutral valuation and the BSM formulas** (Tue)
- f = e^{−rT}Ê[payoff] with μ = r.
- c = S₀N(d₁) − Ke^{−rT}N(d₂); p = Ke^{−rT}N(−d₂) − S₀N(−d₁).
- Example (100, 105, 4%, 25%, 0.75): c = 7.79, p = 9.68; Monte Carlo 7.791 ± 0.014.

**Day 22 — Understanding N(d₁) and N(d₂)** (Wed)
- N(d₂) = P̂(S_T > K) (0.423); N(d₁) = delta (0.509).
- The real-world exercise probability uses μ (0.533 at μ = 12%).
- Limits: large S₀ → forward value; σ → 0 → max(S₀ − Ke^{−rT}, 0).

**Day 23 — Implied volatility and dividends** (Thu)
- Implied vol: find σ so BSM matches the market (bisection). Prices 6 / 9 / 12 → 19.8% / 28.5% / 37.2%.
- Discrete dividends: S₀ → S₀ − PV(D). Continuous yield: S₀ → S₀e^{−qT}.
- Dividends lower calls and raise puts.

**Day 24 — Module C review: BSM three ways** (Fri)
- Tree (n = 500/501 average) 7.7866; formula 7.7865; Monte Carlo 7.791.
- Formula for Europeans, trees for Americans (American put 10.12 vs European 9.69), Monte Carlo for path-dependent payoffs.
- Real-world expected payoff discounted at r: ≈ 11.28, wrong.

## Formula sheet
- BSM PDE: ∂f/∂t + rS ∂f/∂S + ½σ²S² ∂²f/∂S² = rf
- Risk-neutral valuation: f = e^{−rT} Ê[payoff], with dS = rS dt + σS dz
- c = S₀N(d₁) − Ke^{−rT}N(d₂); p = Ke^{−rT}N(−d₂) − S₀N(−d₁)
- d₁ = [ln(S₀/K) + (r + σ²/2)T]/(σ√T); d₂ = d₁ − σ√T
- N(d₂) = risk-neutral P(exercise); N(d₁) = call delta
- Dividends: S₀ → S₀ − PV(D); yield q: S₀ → S₀e^{−qT}, d₁ uses (r − q + σ²/2)
- Put–call parity: c − p = S₀ − Ke^{−rT}

## Solutions

**WC1: A (≈ 98.10).** Deep in the money, N(d₁) ≈ N(d₂) ≈ 1, so c ≈ S₀ − Ke^{−rT}. The exact BSM value is 98.111. B forgets to discount K. C ignores the strike. D is the deep *out*-of-the-money limit.

**WC2.** (a) ln(40/42) = −0.04879; (0.05 + 0.045)(0.5) = 0.0475; σ√T = 0.2121. d₁ = (−0.04879 + 0.0475)/0.2121 = **−0.0061**; d₂ = −0.0061 − 0.2121 = **−0.2182**. (b) Ke^{−rT} = 42e^{−0.025} = 40.963. p = 40.963 × 0.5864 − 40 × 0.5024 = 24.02 − 20.10 = **3.92**. (c) N(d₁) = 1 − 0.5024 = **0.4976**.

**WC3.** Implied vols: K = 90 → **23.4%**, K = 100 → **21.6%**, K = 110 → **21.0%**. Not equal: implied vol *falls* as the strike rises. That's a **volatility skew**, the typical pattern for equities and a sign that real returns aren't exactly lognormal. It's the subject of Module E.
