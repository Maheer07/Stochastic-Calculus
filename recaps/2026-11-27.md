# Week 6 Recap — 2026-11-27 (Days 25–29)

## This week in one paragraph
This was the Greeks week (Module D). It ended with the first look at where BSM breaks (Module E).
- **Monday:** a bank sells a call and has to manage the risk. Naked, covered and stop-loss positions all fail. **Delta hedging** works, with an error that shrinks like 1/√(rebalancing frequency) and a mean of ≈ 0 whatever μ is.
- **Tuesday:** named the two Greeks behind that error, **gamma** (how fast Δ goes stale) and **theta** (time decay). They are tied together by **Θ + rSΔ + ½σ²S²Γ = rΠ**, the BSM PDE in Greek form.
- **Wednesday:** added **vega** and **rho**, and showed that shares can only fix delta. Neutralising gamma and vega needs traded options (a 2×2 solve).
- **Thursday:** how desks really hedge (delta daily, gamma and vega opportunistically) and **scenario grids** for big moves.
- **Friday:** the **volatility smile**. Market implied vols differ by strike, so S_T isn't really lognormal. Next week's Monte Carlo and finite-difference methods are how quants price when formulas run out.

## Day by day
**Day 25 — Delta hedging and the stop-loss strategy** (Mon)
- Sold call (S₀ = 100, K = 105, r = 4%, σ = 25%, T = 0.75) at 7.79; Δ = N(d₁) = 0.509.
- Stop-loss fails: the hedging error's st. dev. is ≈ 0.89 × the price, whatever the frequency.
- Delta hedge performance: 0.31 / 0.16 / 0.07 for rebalancing every 4 weeks / 1 week / 1 day. Error ∝ 1/√(frequency); mean ≈ 0.
- Python: simulate the weekly hedge on 20,000 real-world paths (μ = 8%).

**Day 26 — Theta and gamma** (Tue)
- Θ = −SN′(d₁)σ/(2√T) − rKe^{−rT}N(d₂) = −7.48/yr; Γ = N′(d₁)/(Sσ√T) = 0.0184.
- Θ + rSΔ + ½σ²S²Γ = rΠ. When delta-neutral, Θ ≈ −½σ²S²Γ, so theta and gamma have opposite signs.
- ΔΠ ≈ ΘΔt + ½Γ(ΔS)². The short-gamma book earns $205 a day and breaks even at a ≈ $1.49 move.
- Python: Greeks by formula vs finite differences.

**Day 27 — Vega, rho, and neutralising a portfolio** (Wed)
- ν = S√T N′(d₁) = 34.5 (+0.345 per vol point); ρ_call = KTe^{−rT}N(d₂) = 32.3.
- Shares have Γ = ν = 0, so you need one traded option per extra Greek. Fix delta last, with shares.
- Short 10,000 calls: buy 1,944 A + 7,732 B, then 434 shares, giving Δ = Γ = ν = 0.
- Python: `np.linalg.solve` for the 2×2 hedge.

**Day 28 — Hedging in practice and scenario analysis** (Thu)
- Practice: delta at least daily; gamma and vega monitored and fixed when an opportunity arises.
- Greeks are local (a Taylor expansion), so also fully reprice on an (S, σ) grid.
- Delta-only worst cell −25,987 vs full hedge −788 (≈ 33× smaller).
- Python: a scenario grid by full BSM revaluation.

**Day 29 — Volatility smiles** (Fri)
- Implied vol varies with K. By put–call parity, c_mkt − c_BSM = p_mkt − p_BSM, so calls and puts share one smile.
- FX: a smile (both tails fat). Equities: a skew (leverage, crashophobia).
- A 5% chance of a 30% crash gives a skew of 23.6% (K = 80) → 20.9% (K = 120). No crash gives a flat 20%.
- Python: Monte Carlo prices plus bisection for implied vols.

## Formula sheet
- Δ_call = N(d₁); Δ_put = N(d₁) − 1
- Γ = N′(d₁)/(Sσ√T) (call = put); N′(x) = e^{−x²/2}/√(2π)
- Θ_call = −SN′(d₁)σ/(2√T) − rKe^{−rT}N(d₂)
- ν = S√T N′(d₁) (call = put); ρ_call = KTe^{−rT}N(d₂)
- Θ + rSΔ + ½σ²S²Γ = rΠ
- ΔΠ ≈ Δ·ΔS + ½Γ(ΔS)² + ν·Δσ + Θ·Δt
- Hedging error ∝ 1/√(rebalancing frequency)
- Gamma-neutral with option A: w = −Γ_Π/Γ_A; then shares = −(Δ_Π + wΔ_A)
- Smile: c_mkt − c_BSM = p_mkt − p_BSM ⇒ the same implied vol for a call and a put at the same (K, T)

## Solutions

**WC1: A (−$1,500).** ΔΠ ≈ ν·Δσ = −50,000 × 0.03 = −500 × 3 = **−$1,500**. B has the wrong sign: short vega loses when vol rises. C uses Δσ = 3 instead of 0.03. D: delta and gamma hedges do nothing for vega.

**WC2.** (a) Differentiate parity with respect to S: Δ_put = Δ_call − 1 = N(d₁) − 1. Differentiate again: Γ_put = Γ_call, because the −1 has zero derivative. With respect to σ: the S and Ke^{−rT} terms don't depend on σ, so ν_put = ν_call. (b) Δ_put = **−0.4914**, Γ = **0.0184**, ν = **34.54**. (c) Short 10,000 puts has delta = −10,000 × (−0.4914) = **+4,914**, so **sell 4,914 shares**. The book is **short gamma** (−184), just like short calls.

**WC3.** Seed 1, 1M paths: puts K = 80 / 90 / 100 → **0.479 / 1.795 / 5.067**, with implied vols **23.63% / 22.31% / 21.52%**. These are identical to the call implied vols at the same strikes. Because the simulated S_T is rescaled so that Ê[S_T] = S₀e^{rT}, the Monte Carlo call and put prices satisfy put–call parity exactly. So c_mkt − c_BSM = p_mkt − p_BSM at every σ, and the implied vols must agree.
