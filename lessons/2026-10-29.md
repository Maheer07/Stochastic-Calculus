### Recall — Module A in five lines
1. Decisions at time t use only what's known at time t.
2. ln S_T is normal, so S_T is lognormal: ln S_T ~ φ[ln S₀ + (μ − σ²/2)T, σ²T].
3. Price = cost of a riskless copy: Δ = (f_u − f_d)/(S₀u − S₀d).
4. Equivalently, price = e^{−rT} × expected payoff under p = (e^{rΔt} − d)/(u − d). The real μ drops out.
5. Multi-step trees: backward induction, with an early-exercise check for American options and u = e^{σ√Δt}.

Today ties these together and asks: *what happens to the tree as Δt → 0?*

### Today's concept
*Hull: "Binomial Trees" — appendix "Derivation of the Black–Scholes–Merton Option-Pricing Formula from a Binomial Tree" (skim it); next module: "Wiener Processes and Itô's Lemma".*

**The tree in log space.** With u = e^{σ√Δt} and d = 1/u, every step adds **+σ√Δt or −σ√Δt** to ln S. After n steps with k ups:
  ln(S_T/S₀) = (2k − n) σ√Δt.
So the log-price is a **random walk**: a sum of many small, independent ±steps.

**Shrink the steps.** Fix T = 1, σ = 0.2, r = 4% and let n grow (risk-neutral p):
| steps n | p | mean of ln(S_T/S₀) | st. dev. |
|---|---|---|---|
| 4 | 0.5252 | 0.0202 | 0.1997 |
| 52 (weekly) | 0.5069 | 0.0200 | 0.2000 |
| 1000 | 0.5016 | 0.0200 | 0.2000 |
| limit | ½ | (r − σ²/2)T = **0.02** | σ√T = **0.20** |

Three things to notice:
- **p → ½**, but the tiny tilt above ½ supplies exactly the drift (r − σ²/2)T. This is Day 2's formula, with μ replaced by r (risk-neutral world, Day 5).
- **Each step's size is σ√Δt, not σΔt.** If moves shrank like Δt, total variance n(σΔt)² = σ²TΔt → 0, and the stock would stop being random. Moves of size √Δt keep the variance at σ²T however finely we slice time.
- By the central limit theorem (Day 2), the sum of the ±steps becomes **normal**: the tree's S_T becomes lognormal.

**What the limit is called.** The ± random walk with steps √Δt, in the limit, is Hull's **Wiener process** z: over a small step Δt it moves by
  **Δz = ε√Δt**, with ε ~ N(0, 1).
That's tomorrow's starting point. Module B builds on it to get dS = μS dt + σS dz and then Black–Scholes.

**Code** (simulate the tree's endpoint directly):
```python
import numpy as np
r, sigma, T, S0, n = 0.04, 0.2, 1.0, 100, 250
dt = T / n
u = np.exp(sigma*np.sqrt(dt)); d = 1/u
p = (np.exp(r*dt) - d) / (u - d)                 # risk-neutral probability
rng = np.random.default_rng(42)
k = rng.binomial(n, p, size=100_000)             # number of up-moves on each path
log_ret = (2*k - n) * sigma*np.sqrt(dt)          # ln(S_T / S0) for each path
print(log_ret.mean(), log_ret.std())             # ≈ 0.020, 0.200
```

> **Common pitfall.** Thinking a finer tree needs smaller *volatility*. σ stays the same; only the step size σ√Δt shrinks. And the √ is what keeps the randomness alive as Δt → 0.
