### Today's concept
*Hull: "The Black–Scholes–Merton Model" — "Volatility" and "Estimating Volatility from Historical Data", including the box on trading days vs calendar days.*

**Recipe (Hull).** Observe prices S₀, S₁, …, S_n at regular intervals of τ years (τ = 1/252 for daily data).
1. Log returns: **uᵢ = ln(Sᵢ / Sᵢ₋₁)**.
2. Sample st. dev.: **s = √[ Σ(uᵢ − ū)² / (n − 1) ]**.
3. Since s ≈ σ√τ (Day 9), the estimate is **σ̂ = s / √τ**. For daily data, σ̂ = s × √252.
4. Its standard error ≈ **σ̂ / √(2n)**.

**Worked example** (11 daily closing prices, so 10 returns):
100, 101.2, 100.5, 102.1, 101.0, 103.3, 102.6, 104.0, 103.1, 104.8, 105.5
- u = 0.0119, −0.0069, 0.0158, −0.0108, 0.0225, −0.0068, 0.0136, −0.0087, 0.0164, 0.0067
- s = **0.01245** (1.245% a day)
- σ̂ = 0.01245 × √252 = **19.8%** a year
- standard error = 19.8%/√20 = **4.4%**. So the honest answer is "about 20%, give or take 9%". Ten days is not much data.

**How much data?** More observations shrink the error like 1/√n, but old data may not reflect today's volatility. Hull's rule of thumb: use the last **90–180 days** of daily prices, or set the window equal to the option's life (e.g. 2 years of data for a 2-year option).

**Trading days vs calendar days.** Volatility is much higher when the exchange is open than when it's closed (studies of weekends show this). So volatility is measured per **trading day** and annualised with **252**, not 365. An option's life is likewise measured in trading days: T = (trading days to expiry)/252.

**Code** (estimate σ from simulated prices whose true σ is 20%):
```python
import numpy as np
rng = np.random.default_rng(42)
dt = 1/252
u = 0.06*dt + 0.2*np.sqrt(dt)*rng.standard_normal(1260)   # 5 years of daily log returns
for n in (21, 252, 1260):                                   # 1 month, 1 year, 5 years
    sig_hat = u[:n].std(ddof=1) * np.sqrt(252)             # ddof=1 → divide by n − 1
    print(n, round(sig_hat, 4), round(sig_hat/np.sqrt(2*n), 4))   # estimate and its st. error
```
Output: 1 month → **17.0% ± 2.6%**; 1 year → **18.8% ± 0.8%**; 5 years → **19.8% ± 0.4%**. The estimate homes in on 20% as data accumulates.

> **Common pitfall.** Annualising daily vol by ×252 (variance thinking applied to st. dev.) or by ×√365 (calendar days). Use **×√252**: 1.2% daily → 19.0% a year, not 302% and not 22.9%.
