### Recall
Delta hedging leaves an error that shrinks like 1/√(frequency) (Day 25). Today we name the two Greeks behind it. **Gamma** measures how fast Δ goes stale; **theta** is what you're paid (or pay) for carrying that risk while time passes.

### Today's concept
*Hull: "The Greek Letters" — "Theta", "Gamma" and "Relationship Between Delta, Theta, and Gamma".*

**Theta (Θ) = ∂Π/∂t**: how the value changes as time passes, with S fixed. For a European call (no dividends):
  **Θ = −S N′(d₁)σ / (2√T) − rKe^{−rT}N(d₂)**,
where N′(x) = e^{−x²/2}/√(2π) is the normal density (the bell curve). Θ is usually **negative** for a long option: time decay.

**Gamma (Γ) = ∂²Π/∂S² = ∂Δ/∂S**: how fast delta changes as S moves.
  **Γ = N′(d₁) / (S σ √T)** (the same for calls and puts).
Γ is largest near the money and shrinks deep in or out of the money.

*Our call* (S₀ = 100, K = 105, r = 4%, σ = 25%, T = 0.75): c = 7.787, Δ = 0.5086, **Γ = 0.0184**, **Θ = −7.48 per year** (−0.0205 per calendar day; Hull divides by 365).

**The link: the BSM equation in Greek clothing.** Put ∂f/∂t = Θ, ∂f/∂S = Δ and ∂²f/∂S² = Γ into the BSM PDE (Day 20):
  **Θ + rSΔ + ½σ²S²Γ = rΠ**.
*Check:* −7.480 + 0.04 × 100 × 0.5086 + ½ × 0.0625 × 10⁴ × 0.0184 = **0.3115** = 0.04 × 7.787 ✓.

For a **delta-neutral** portfolio (Δ = 0) this becomes Θ + ½σ²S²Γ = rΠ. With Π small, **Θ ≈ −½σ²S²Γ**: theta and gamma have *opposite signs*.
- **Long gamma** (you own options): you profit from big moves but bleed theta every day.
- **Short gamma** (you sold options, like the bank on Day 25): you collect theta but lose on big moves.

**Where Day 25's hedging error comes from.** Over a short Δt a delta-neutral position changes by
  **ΔΠ ≈ Θ Δt + ½ Γ (ΔS)²**  (Taylor expansion, Day 14).
Short 10,000 calls, delta-hedged:
- Theta earns **+$205 per day**.
- An overnight $3 move costs ½ × 10,000 × 0.0184 × 9 = **−$829**.
- The break-even daily move is √(2|Θ|Δt/Γ) ≈ **$1.49**. Smaller moves, you win; bigger ones, you lose.

On average they cancel, which is why the hedge's mean P&L was ≈ 0.

**Code** (Greeks by formula vs finite differences, and the identity):
```python
from math import log, sqrt, exp, erf, pi
N  = lambda x: 0.5*(1 + erf(x/sqrt(2)))
Nd = lambda x: exp(-x*x/2)/sqrt(2*pi)                       # normal density N'(x)
S, K, r, sigma, T = 100, 105, 0.04, 0.25, 0.75
def call(S, T):
    d1 = (log(S/K) + (r + sigma**2/2)*T)/(sigma*sqrt(T)); return S*N(d1) - K*exp(-r*T)*N(d1 - sigma*sqrt(T))
d1 = (log(S/K) + (r + sigma**2/2)*T)/(sigma*sqrt(T)); d2 = d1 - sigma*sqrt(T)
delta, gamma = N(d1), Nd(d1)/(S*sigma*sqrt(T))
theta = -S*Nd(d1)*sigma/(2*sqrt(T)) - r*K*exp(-r*T)*N(d2)
print(gamma, (call(S+0.01, T) - 2*call(S, T) + call(S-0.01, T))/0.01**2)   # 0.01842 both
print(theta + r*S*delta + 0.5*sigma**2*S**2*gamma, r*call(S, T))         # 0.3115 both
```

> **Common pitfall.** Thinking theta is "free money" for option sellers. Theta is the *fair payment* for being short gamma. On a day with a big move, the gamma loss wipes out many days of theta. The identity says that, on average, they offset exactly.
